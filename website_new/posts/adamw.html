Understanding AdamW via Worst-Case Convergence Analysis

Dmitry Kovalev·November 25, 2026
\newcommand{\R}{\mathbb{R}} \newcommand{\E}{\mathbb{E}} \newcommand{\cD}{\mathcal{D}}

Before the invention of Muon [JJB+24], AdamW [LH19] has been arguably the most popular optimizer for large-scale deep learning. Its fast and stable convergence made it a default choice in most practical scenarios. Unfortunately, despite the fact that this algorithm was invented almost a decade ago, its theoretical understanding remains limited. In this post, we will try to find a theoretical justification of AdamW's empirical success by means of theoretical worst-case convergence analysis. More concretely, we will show that AdamW may be the best possible algorithm for a certain class of stochastic convex optimization problems.

Preliminaries

Stochastic Optimization

Given a stochastic objective function \(f_\xi(\cdot)\colon \R^d \to \R\), where \(\xi\) is a random variable sampled from a probability distribution \(\cD\), consider a standard stochastic optimization problem:

\[ \label{eq:main} \min_{x \in \R^d} f(x),\quad\text{where}\quad f(x) = \E_{\xi \sim \cD}[f_\xi(x)]. \]

One of the simplest algorithms for solving such problems is SGD with a constant stepsize \(\eta > 0\), which iterates

\[ x_{k+1} = x_k - \eta \cdot \nabla f_{\xi_k}(x_k), \]

where \(\xi_k \sim \cD\) is sampled independently at each iteration. Under mild regularity assumptions, the stochastic gradient is unbiased, i.e.,

\[ \E_{\xi_k \sim \cD} [\nabla f_{\xi_k}(x_k)] = \nabla f(x_k), \]

so that SGD coincides with GD in expectation.

AdamW

When applied to this problem, AdamW can be described with the following pseudocode.

Algorithm 1: AdamW

  • For \(k = 1,2, \dots, K-1\)
    • obtain stochastic gradient:
      \(g_k = \nabla f_{\xi_k}(x)\), where \(\xi_k \sim \cD\) is a fresh sample
    • compute 1st moment:
      \(m_k = \beta_1 m_{k-1} + (1-\beta_1) g_k\)
    • compute 2nd moment:
      \(v_k = \beta_2 v_{k-1} + (1-\beta_2) (g_k \odot g_k)\)
    • apply bias correction:
      \(\hat{m}_k = m_k/(1-\beta_1^k)\), \(\hat{v}_k = v_k/(1-\beta_2^k)\)
    • perform update with componentwise preconditioning and decoupled weight decay:
      \(x_{k+1} = (1-\eta\lambda)x_k - \eta \cdot \hat{m}_k\oslash\sqrt{\hat v_k}\)
  • Return \(x_K\)
